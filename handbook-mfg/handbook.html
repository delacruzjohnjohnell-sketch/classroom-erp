1 · SIGN IN · CHOOSE ERP · COMPANY

Getting Started

The Manufacturing ERP runs beside the Standard ERP inside the same Classroom ERP site. You sign in once, then pick which one to work in.

Signing in and choosing the ERP

  1. Open classroom-erp.vercel.app and sign in with the school email your teacher gave you.
  2. On the chooser page pick Manufacturing ERP (the Standard ERP is the trading/service version).
  3. Your teacher decides when Manufacturing is open to students. If the card is greyed out, ask your teacher to open it.

Your company

Everything you do belongs to one company. There are two kinds:

KindWho creates itWho works in it
Solo companyYou, the first time you open the Manufacturing ERP (one per student)Only you. You hold every role.
Team companyThe teacher, from Team companiesA team of students. Each member holds one or more roles (see the Teams and Roles chapter).

The company switcher in the top bar lists every company you can open. The company name and your name are always visible there so you know whose books you are in.

The screen layout

  • Left sidebar — one entry per module, in the order of the manufacturing cycle (Master Data → Costing → Planning → Purchasing → Inventory → Production → Shop Floor → Quality → Shipping → Sales → Finance → Period End → Analytics, then Banking, Fixed Assets, Payroll, Reports, Settings).
  • Search box — finds customers, vendors, items and invoices across the company.
  • Company switcher — move between the companies you can open (your own, and any team company or answer key you have been given).
  • Switch ERP and Sign out — at the bottom of the sidebar.

How to read the step-by-step boxes: click the bold words (buttons, tabs, menu entries); italic words are boxes you fill in or choices you make.

What is the Manufacturing ERP and a company?

An ERP (enterprise resource planning system) is one program that holds everything a business does: buying, stocking, making, selling and accounting. The big idea is that you type a fact once and every other part of the business sees it. When the warehouse receives bolts, the stock count goes up, the purchase order shows the delivery, and the accountant's books show the new asset, all from one entry.

A company in this system is a complete, separate set of books. Nothing you do in one company shows up in another. That is why each activity should start in a new, empty company: your mistakes in practice never touch a clean one.

A solo company is yours alone and you can do every job in it. A team company is created by the teacher; each member holds roles (for example Purchasing or Accountant) and the system only lets you do what your role allows, just like in a real company.

How to sign in and choose the ERP

  1. Open classroom-erp.vercel.app in your browser.
  2. Type your school email in the Email box and your password in the Password box, then click Sign in.
  3. On the chooser page click the Manufacturing ERP card. (If it is greyed out your teacher has not opened it yet.)
  4. The Manufacturing dashboard opens. The sidebar on the left lists every module.

How to create your own company (first time only)

  1. After choosing Manufacturing ERP for the first time you are asked to create a company.
  2. Type a Company name. Leave Fiscal year starts as it is unless your teacher says otherwise.
  3. Choose the Costing method your teacher told you (Standard costing in the activities).
  4. Click the create button. The new company is empty and ready for Master Data.
NOTE Use a new, empty company for each activity.

How to switch between companies

  1. Click the company switcher in the top bar (it shows the current company name).
  2. Click the company you want in the list. The page reloads with that company's books.

How to load the demo company for practice

  1. Open an empty company. The dashboard offers a demo company.
  2. Click the demo button and wait until the message says the demo company is ready.
  3. Look around: every module now has sample items, orders and postings.

How to open an answer key (after your teacher releases it)

  1. On the Manufacturing dashboard find the answer-key card for the activity you finished.
  2. Click its button to open the key. It opens as a separate read-only company.
  3. Compare each screen with yours, then use the company switcher to go back to your own company.

How to sign out

  1. Click Sign out at the bottom of the left sidebar.
TIP Work from top to bottom through the sidebar the first time. Master data first, then plan, buy, make, ship, bill, and finally close the month.
2 · SIDEBAR → DASHBOARD

Dashboard

The dashboard is the company's health check. It summarises what the books and the factory floor currently say.

What is the dashboard?

The dashboard is the manager's summary page. It does not let you enter data; it shows what the books and the factory floor currently say, so you can decide where to go next. Inventory value is the peso value of everything in stock. Open production orders are jobs that were started but not finished. Pending purchase orders are orders sent to vendors whose goods have not all arrived.

The Needs Your Attention list is a to-do list written by the system: held vendor invoices, quality lots waiting for a decision, items below their safety stock, and so on. If a number there is above zero, somebody has work to do.

How to read the dashboard

  1. Click Dashboard in the left sidebar.
  2. Read the four coloured tiles at the top: Good units made today, Open Production Orders, Inventory Value and Pending Purchase Orders.
  3. Look at Production Output (last 7 days) and Production by Product (last 30 days).
  4. Scroll to Ongoing Production Orders, Inventory Status and Needs Your Attention. A red number means something is waiting for a decision.

How to use Quick Actions

  1. In the right-hand column click one of the tiles: Create Production Order, Add Purchase Order, Adjust Inventory, Enter Vendor Invoice, Ship an Order or Manage BOM.
  2. The matching screen opens so you can start the task at once.

How to open something that needs attention

  1. In Needs Your Attention click the line with a number above zero.
  2. The screen that solves it opens. When the number is back to 0 the line turns green.
  • Key figures — revenue, cost, inventory value and work in process, taken from the same books as the reports.
  • Shortcuts — jump to open purchase orders, production orders, quality lots and unpaid invoices.
  • Recent activity — the latest documents posted by you or your team.
TIP If a dashboard figure surprises you, open Reports → Books integrity check. Every figure comes from the same ledger, so the reports always agree with each other.
3 · SIDEBAR → MASTER DATA

Master Data

Master Data is the dictionary of the factory: what you buy and make, how it is made, where, and at what cost. Set it up before posting any transaction. It has eleven tabs.

Items

Raw materials, packaging, semi-finished and finished goods. Each item has a SKU, a unit, a type, and a standard cost. Finished goods link to a bill of materials and a routing.

What is an item?

An item is anything the company buys, makes or sells: a bolt, a sheet of steel, a half-finished frame, a finished lantern. Everything else in the system points to items, so items are the first thing you create.

Each item has a SKU (a short unique code such as RM-STEEL), a unit of measure (how you count it: pieces, kilograms, meters) and an item type. Raw materials are bought and used up in production. Packaging is bought and used to pack goods. Semi-finished goods are made by the company and then used to make something else (a painted shade that goes into a lantern). Finished goods are what you sell.

Make or buy tells planning how to get more of it: a bought item is ordered from a vendor using its purchase lead time (days the vendor takes); a made item is produced using its production lead time. Safety stock is the cushion you never want to dip below. Planned price is the price you expect to pay a vendor, and it feeds the cost of everything that uses the item. Tick Track batches / lots only when you must know exactly which batch went where, such as food with an expiry date.

How to add an item

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Items tab.
  2. Click Add item.
  3. Fill SKU / code, Name and Unit of measure. Choose the Item type (raw material, packaging, semi-finished or finished good) and Make or buy.
  4. For a bought item fill Purchase lead time (days) and Planned price (₱). For a made item fill Production lead time (days).
  5. If you buy it in another unit, choose it in Buy it in (optional) (the conversion must already exist).
  6. Choose the Usual storage location for semi-finished items. Tick Track batches / lots if the item has lot numbers or expiry dates.
  7. Click Save item.

How to change or delete an item

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Items tab.
  2. Click the edit pencil on the item's row, change what you need and click Save item.
  3. To remove a mistake click the bin on its row and confirm. An item that already has postings cannot be deleted.

Bills of materials

The recipe of a finished good: which components, how many per unit, and the expected scrap. Production orders and planning explode these.

What is a bill of materials (BOM)?

A bill of materials is the recipe of a product. It lists every component needed to make one unit of an item, and how many of each. A lantern might need 1 frame, 1 shade, 2 screws and 1 box. Without a BOM the system cannot calculate what a product costs, what to buy, or what to take out of stock when you produce.

A BOM can have several levels. If the shade is itself made from paper and paint, the shade has its own BOM. The system follows these levels automatically when it explodes a BOM, which means listing everything needed down to raw materials.

Quantity made is the number of finished units the recipe produces (normally 1). Scrap % adds an allowance for material that is normally wasted. Valid from is the date the recipe starts to apply, so a change in the recipe next month does not rewrite last month's costs. A BOM starts as a draft, which you can still edit, and only becomes usable when you Activate it. An active BOM is never edited; you create a new version instead, so history is kept.

How to create a bill of materials (BOM)

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Bills of materials tab.
  2. Click New BOM.
  3. Under the header choose Item this makes, leave Alternative at 1, type Quantity made (usually 1) and Valid from (the first day the recipe applies).
  4. Click Add line for each component: choose the Item and type the Quantity per unit made (the unit fills in by itself). Use Scrap % only if the set tells you to.
  5. Click Save draft.
  6. Check the lines, then click Activate and confirm. Only an active BOM is used by costing, planning and production.
NOTE An active BOM cannot be edited. To change a recipe click New version, edit the new draft, Save draft and Activate it.

How to revise a BOM (new version)

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Bills of materials tab.
  2. Click the BOM's number to open it, then click New version.
  3. Change the lines and set Valid from to the date the change starts.
  4. Click Save draft, then Activate. Work out the new standard cost afterwards (see the Costing chapter).

How to explode a BOM or see where an item is used

  1. Open the BOM (click its number).
  2. Type the Quantity to make and the date, then click Explode to see every component needed at every level.
  3. In the where-used section choose direct uses or all levels up to see which parents contain the item.

Routings

The sequence of operations for a product, the work centre used by each, and the setup and run time per unit.

What is a routing?

A routing is the method of making a product: the steps, in order, with the machine or work area used for each and the time each step takes. If the BOM says what goes into a product, the routing says how and where it is made.

Each operation has a setup time (getting the machine ready, spent once per batch), a run time per unit, and the labor and machine minutes per unit. These minutes are multiplied by the hourly rates to give labor and machine cost, and they also tell planning how many hours the work centers need. Like a BOM, a routing is a draft until you Activate it.

How to create a routing

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Routings tab.
  2. Click New routing.
  3. Choose the item the routing makes.
  4. Click Add operation for each step: type What is done, choose the Work center, then fill setup, run, labor and machine minutes.
  5. Click Save draft, then Activate and confirm.

Work centers

Machines or labour groups where operations are done, with capacity and the cost rates that production confirmations apply.

What is a work center?

A work center is a place where work is done: a cutting machine, an assembly bench, a paint booth. It is the unit of capacity. The system needs to know how many hours it can run to warn you when you plan more work than it can handle.

Shifts per day and hours per shift give the raw hours. Utilization % is the share of those hours the center is really working (breaks and waiting excluded). Efficiency % says how fast it runs compared with the standard time. Available hours is the result: how many productive hours the center can give in a period, after holidays and non-working days.

How to add a work center

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Work centers tab.
  2. Click Add work center.
  3. Type the Name. Leave shifts, hours, utilization, efficiency and working days as they are unless told otherwise; choose a cost center if you use them.
  4. Click Save.

How to find the hours a work center can run

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Work centers tab.
  2. Scroll to the available-hours box. Choose the work center, then the From and To dates.
  3. Click Calculate. The hours account for shifts, utilization, working days and holidays.

Activity rates

The planned peso rates per labour hour, machine hour and overhead basis used for standard costing.

What is an activity rate?

An activity rate is the price of one hour of work. There are two kinds: direct labor (a worker's hour) and machine time (a machine's hour, covering power, wear and upkeep). When production confirms hours worked, the system multiplies them by these rates to charge cost to the order.

Rates are set per work center and per month, which is why the start date must be the first of a month. Changing a rate for next month does not change cost already booked in earlier months.

How to add an activity rate (peso per labor or machine hour)

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Activity rates tab.
  2. Click Add rate.
  3. Choose the work center and the activity (direct labor or machine time).
  4. Type the first day of the month the rate starts (it must be the 1st) and the rate per hour in pesos.
  5. Click Save. Repeat for every work center and activity.

Calendar

Working days and holidays. Planning and scheduling skip non-working days.

What is the working calendar?

The calendar tells planning which days work happens. Weekends are handled by each work center's working days; the calendar adds holidays on top. Planning and scheduling skip these days, so a lamp promised for a holiday is scheduled for the next working day.

How to add a holiday

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Calendar tab.
  2. Click Add holiday, pick the Date and type a Name.
  3. Click Save. Planning and scheduling skip that day.

Units and Conversions

Units of measure (pc, kg, sheet…) and the factors that convert one into another, for example buying in boxes and consuming in pieces.

What is a unit of measure?

A unit of measure is how a quantity is counted: piece, kilogram, liter, meter, box. Every item has one stock unit and all stock quantities are kept in it. Create each unit once, then choose it on items.

How to add a unit of measure

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Units tab.
  2. Click Add unit.
  3. Type the Code (for example KG) and the Name (for example Kilogram).
  4. Click Save.

What is a unit conversion?

A conversion lets you buy in one unit and stock in another. A vendor may sell string by the bundle while you use it by the meter. If one bundle is 50 meters, you create the conversion 1 BDL = 50 M. You then order bundles; the system receives meters into stock, and the cost per meter is worked out for you. Without the conversion the system would not know how many meters arrived.

How to add a unit conversion (buy in bundles, stock in kilograms)

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Conversions tab.
  2. Click Add conversion.
  3. Choose what it Applies to, then fill the unit you buy in, the factor, and the stock unit.
  4. Click Save.

Locations

Warehouses and bins where stock is kept. Every stock figure is per item per location.

What is a storage location?

A location is a place where stock sits: raw materials store, work-in-process area, finished goods store, quarantine. Stock is always counted per item and per location. Quarantine is special: goods that must be inspected are received there first and are not usable until quality accepts them.

How to add a storage location

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Locations tab.
  2. Click Add location.
  3. Type the Code and Name, and choose its kind (raw materials, finished goods, quarantine…).
  4. Click Save.

Cost centers

The departments to which costs are charged and reported.

What is a cost center?

A cost center is a department that costs are charged to and reported by, such as Production, Warehouse or Administration. Work centers can be linked to one, so reports can show what each department spent.

How to add a cost center

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Cost centers tab.
  2. Click Add cost center, type the Code and Name.
  3. Click Save.

Costing sheet

The layout that builds a product's cost: materials, labour, machine, overhead and the surcharges applied to each.

What is the costing sheet?

The costing sheet is the rule that adds overhead to a product's cost. Overhead is cost that cannot be pointed to one unit directly, such as factory rent, supervision or electricity. The sheet says, for example, add ₱20 for every labor hour. That way each product carries a fair share of the factory's indirect cost, not only its materials and wages.

How to set the fixed overhead rate (costing sheet)

  1. Click Master Data in the left sidebar, then the Costing sheet tab.
  2. Find the overhead row and click its edit pencil.
  3. Type the pesos per hour and choose what it is counted on (for example labor hours).
  4. Click Save. To add another surcharge click Add surcharge instead.
NOTE Your teacher can load a ready-made demo company into your company for practice. Changing master data (such as a standard cost) is recorded in the audit trail.
4 · SIDEBAR → COSTING

Costing

Costing answers 'what should one unit cost?' before you make it. A cost estimate prices a lot of a product from its bill of materials, routing and activity rates.

What is a cost estimate and the standard cost?

A cost estimate answers: what should one unit of this product cost? The system takes the active BOM (materials at planned prices), the active routing (hours times activity rates) and the costing sheet (overhead), and adds them up. It does this for the lot size you choose, because setup time is spread over the units in a batch.

When you release the estimate it becomes the item's standard cost: the fixed, planned cost per unit. Under standard costing stock is always valued at the standard, and when production really costs more or less the difference is posted as a variance. Variances are how management learns whether the factory is working better or worse than planned.

Always cost the inner levels first (semi-finished items), because the cost of a finished good includes the standard cost of the parts inside it.

How to work out and release a standard cost

  1. Click Costing in the left sidebar.
  2. Click New estimate.
  3. Choose the Item to cost, type the Lot size and the costing date (the active BOM and routing on that date are used).
  4. Click Work out the cost. Read the unit cost and any warnings (a missing price or rate).
  5. Click Mark this cost.
  6. Click Release as standard cost and confirm. The entry shown under Journal entry this will post revalues stock.
NOTE Cost the semi-finished items (shades, bases) before the finished goods that use them.

How to check whether a standard cost is still right

  1. Click Costing in the left sidebar.
  2. Type the percentage difference to flag.
  3. Click Check the standard costs. Items whose current cost has drifted show with the difference.

How to see what an estimate contains

  1. Click Costing in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the estimate's number in the list.
  3. Read where the cost comes from and every cost line.
  4. Click All estimates to go back.
  1. Click New estimate.
  2. Choose the item, the lot size and the costing date.
  3. Review the unit cost and any warnings (a missing price or rate shows here).
  4. Release the estimate so it can serve as the item's standard cost when the company uses Standard costing.
ColumnMeaning

Unit cost / Lot costResult per piece and for the whole lot.

5 · SIDEBAR → PLANNING (MRP)

Planning

Planning looks at demand and stock, then tells you what to make and what to buy, and when. It has seven views.

Planned orders

Suggested production orders: item, quantity, start and finish dates and how many days late each is.

What is planning (MRP) and a planned order?

MRP (material requirements planning) is the system's way of answering three questions: what do we need, how much, and by when? It looks at demand (confirmed sales orders and forecasts), subtracts what you already have and what is already on order, explodes the BOMs, and proposes what to make and what to buy.

A planned order is a proposal to produce something. It is only a suggestion; nothing is committed until you convert it into a production order. A planned order you move or create yourself becomes firm, meaning the next planning run will keep it instead of replacing it.

How to run planning (MRP)

  1. Click Planning in the left sidebar.
  2. Set Plan from (the first date to plan) and Look ahead (how far).
  3. Tick the forecast option if you want forecast demand counted as well as sales orders.
  4. Click Run planning. Suggestions nobody has converted are replaced by the new ones.
  5. Read the counts of planned orders to review, requisitions to buy and exceptions.

How to turn planned orders into production orders

  1. Click Planning in the left sidebar.
  2. Open the planned-orders view.
  3. To make one order click Make it on its row.
  4. To make several tick their boxes and click Make selected. It is all or none.
  5. Check them under Production in the sidebar.

How to move or delete a planned order

  1. In the planned orders click Move on the row, type the new start and finish dates and confirm. The order becomes firm: the next run keeps it.
  2. To remove a suggestion click Delete on its row.

Requisitions

Suggested purchases: the item, quantity, the date to order by and the date it is needed.

What is a purchase requisition?

A requisition is the proposal to buy: item, quantity, the date to order by (which already allows for the vendor's lead time) and the date it is needed. You turn requisitions into a real purchase order for one vendor at a time.

How to turn requisitions into a purchase order

  1. Click Planning in the left sidebar.
  2. Open the requisitions view.
  3. Tick the boxes of the requisitions for one vendor.
  4. Click Create purchase order….
  5. Choose the vendor (or type a new vendor's name), check Needed on and type the price of each line.
  6. Click Create purchase order. Receive the goods later on the Purchasing page.

How to delete a requisition

  1. In the requisitions view click Delete on the row (for example when you order that item by hand in another unit).

Exceptions

Messages that need a decision, such as an order that is late or a requirement that cannot be met.

What is an exception message and the planning views?

An exception is a message from planning that something needs a human decision: an order that will finish late, demand that cannot be met in time, or an order that is no longer needed. Treat the list as the planner's daily to-do list.

Capacity compares the hours the plan needs against the hours each work center has; a red bar means overload and you must move work or add shifts. The planning grid shows week by week the demand, the supply and the projected stock of one item, so you can see exactly when a shortage will happen. The Gantt draws orders as bars on a calendar.

How to read exceptions, capacity, the grid and the Gantt

  1. Exceptions: each line is a decision (an order that is late, a need that cannot be met). Click a row to open that item's planning grid.
  2. Capacity: hours needed against hours available per work center; red means overload.
  3. Planning grid: choose an item to see demand, supply and projected stock week by week.
  4. Gantt: a timeline of planned and released orders.

Forecast

Expected future sales per item, which planning uses together with real sales orders.

What is a forecast?

A forecast is the sales you expect but have no order for yet. When you tick Include the forecast in a planning run, the system also prepares material and production for these expected sales, so the factory is ready before the order arrives.

How to enter a forecast

  1. Click Planning in the left sidebar.
  2. Open the forecast view.
  3. Choose the Item, the Month and the expected sales (type 0 to clear one).
  4. Click Save forecast.

Capacity

Hours required against hours available per work centre per period, so you can see overload before it happens.

Planning grid

Week-by-week projected stock for each item: demand, supply and projected balance.

Gantt

A timeline of the planned and released orders.

TIP Convert a planned order into a production order, and a requisition into a purchase order, instead of re-typing them.
6 · SIDEBAR → PURCHASING

Purchasing

Buying from vendors: create the purchase order, receive the goods into stock, then enter the vendor's invoice.

What is a purchase order and goods receipt?

A purchase order (PO) is a written promise to a vendor to buy items at agreed prices. It does not change the books by itself; it simply records what you expect to receive. When the goods really arrive you post a goods receipt. Now stock goes up, and the books record a liability called goods received, not invoiced (GR/IR), because you have the goods but the vendor has not billed you yet.

You can receive in parts. A PO is partially received until every line has arrived. Goods that need inspection are received into the quarantine location, and quality lots appear automatically.

How to create a purchase order by hand

  1. Click Purchasing in the left sidebar.
  2. Click New purchase order.
  3. Choose the Vendor (or type a new name). Set the order date and the expected date.
  4. Choose the item, type Qty and the price each. If you buy in bundles, choose that unit on the line. Click Add line for more items.
  5. Check the total, then click Create order.

How to delete a purchase order

  1. Click Delete on a purchase order that has no receipts and confirm.

How to receive goods into stock

  1. Click Purchasing in the left sidebar.
  2. Click Receive on the purchase order's row.
  3. Set the receipt date.
  4. In the Now column type the quantity that arrived for each line (leave the rest as 0 for a partial delivery). Choose where it goes Into. For an inspected item choose the QUARANTINE store.
  5. For a lot-tracked item fill the lot number and expiry date.
  6. Click Receive into stock.
  1. New purchase order — pick the vendor, items, quantities and prices.
  2. Receive into stock — when goods arrive, record the receipt. Stock goes up and the books show 'Goods received / invoice received'.
  3. Enter the vendor invoice. The system does a three-way match of order, receipt and invoice; a difference above the tolerance is held for the teacher.
  4. Pay the vendor with Pay on the invoice (Banking is not needed).

What is the vendor invoice and the three-way match?

The vendor invoice is the bill from the vendor. Before the company accepts it, the system performs a three-way match: it compares the purchase order (what you ordered and the agreed price), the goods receipt (what actually arrived) and the invoice (what the vendor charges). If all three agree, the invoice is posted and you owe the vendor. If the quantity or price differs by more than the tolerance in Settings, the invoice goes on hold and a responsible person must release or reject it.

Landed cost is the extra cost of getting goods to your door: freight, customs duty, insurance. You add it to the receipts it belongs to and the system spreads it across the goods by value, weight or volume, so each item's cost includes its share. Payment is recorded with Pay, which reduces what you owe the vendor.

How to enter the vendor's invoice (three-way match)

  1. Click Purchasing in the left sidebar.
  2. Scroll to the vendor invoices and click Enter vendor invoice.
  3. Choose the purchase order. The lines show what was ordered, what arrived and what is already invoiced.
  4. Type the vendor's number in Their invoice number, the invoice date, the due date and the VAT %.
  5. Type the billed quantity and billed price from the invoice (the quantity that has arrived is filled in for you).
  6. Click Match and post. If it agrees within the tolerance it is posted and you owe the vendor; if not it goes ON HOLD.
NOTE Enter the invoice after the goods and any replacement delivery have been received.

How to deal with an invoice on hold

  1. Find the invoice with the status On hold in the vendor invoices; the Why column tells you the difference.
  2. To accept the difference, the teacher or (in a team company) the Finance manager clicks Release. The person who entered the invoice cannot release it.
  3. To refuse it click Reject, type the reason and confirm. Then enter a corrected invoice with Enter vendor invoice.

How to pay a vendor invoice

  1. In the vendor invoices click Pay on the posted invoice.
  2. Check the amount (the balance is filled in; type less for a part payment), the date and the method.
  3. Click the record-payment button.

How to void a vendor invoice entered by mistake

  1. Click Void on the invoice (only while it has no payment).
  2. Type the date and the reason and confirm. Then enter the right invoice.

How to add landed cost (freight, customs)

  1. Click Purchasing in the left sidebar.

Inventory

Where your stock is. Every movement is a numbered stock document and writes its journal voucher automatically.

Stock

What is stock on hand?

Stock on hand is the quantity of each item in each location, and its peso value. The value comes from the item's cost (standard cost under standard costing). Every change of stock is a numbered stock document, and each document writes its own journal voucher so the books always agree with the warehouse.

How to see what is in stock

  1. Click Inventory in the left sidebar.
  2. The Stock on hand tab lists every item by location with its value.
  3. Click Availability to see usable, reserved and projected stock.

Availability

What is on hand, reserved and expected, so you know what can still be promised.

What is availability?

Availability separates what you have from what you can still promise. On hand is physically in stock. Reserved is already committed to open orders. Projected adds what is expected from purchase and production orders. Short means you cannot cover demand; over-promised means orders exceed everything you have and expect.

Lots

Batches with expiry dates for traceability, from supplier lot through to customer delivery.

What is a lot (batch)?

A lot or batch is a group of units that share the same origin, such as one delivery from a vendor or one production run. Lot-tracked items carry a lot number and an expiry date. If a vendor reports a defective batch, trace tells you where every unit went: backward to find the source, forward to find the customers who received it.

How to find a lot and trace it

  1. Click Inventory in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Lots & expiry tab.
  3. Choose the lot and the direction (backward shows where it came from, forward where it went).
  4. Click Trace.

Documents

The list of every stock document (receipt, issue, transfer, delivery, adjustment) and the voucher it wrote.

What is a stock document?

A stock document is the numbered record of one movement: receipt, issue to production, transfer, delivery, adjustment. You never delete a posted document. If it was wrong, you reverse it: the system posts an opposite document, and both stay on record. This is how real accounting keeps an honest trail.

How to reverse a stock document

  1. Click Inventory in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Stock documents tab.
  3. Click Reverse on the document, type the date and the reason, then confirm.
  4. The original stays on the record and shows Reversed; a new document undoes it.

Move stock

Transfer between locations, adjust after a count, or scrap.

What is an adjustment and a transfer?

An adjustment changes the quantity in the books to match reality: an opening balance when you start using the system, or a stock count difference when the physical count is not what the system says. A transfer only moves stock from one location to another and changes nothing in the books.

How to post an opening balance or a stock count difference

  1. Click Inventory in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Adjust and transfer tab.
  3. Choose What: opening balance (adds stock) or stock count difference.
  4. Choose the Item and Where. Type the change in quantity (a minus sign takes stock away) and the cost of one unit.
  5. Set the date. For a lot-tracked item also type the lot number and expiry.
  6. Click Post adjustment.

How to move stock between locations

  1. On the Adjust and transfer tab go to the move-stock box.
  2. Choose the item, From, the destination, the quantity and the date.
  3. Click Move stock. Nothing is posted to the books; only the location changes.
TIP Stock cannot go below zero unless your teacher allows it in Settings. If an issue is refused, check the stock first.
8 · SIDEBAR → PRODUCTION

Production

Making things. A production order consumes materials, labour and machine time and produces finished goods.

What is a production order?

A production order is the instruction to make a quantity of a product. It copies the BOM (materials) and the routing (operations) at that moment. Its life has steps: released (work may start) → materials issued (stock moves into work in process) → operations confirmed (hours and good/scrap quantities recorded) → finished goods received → settled → closed.

Work in process (WIP) is the cost of jobs that are started but not yet finished goods: materials already issued plus labor and machine cost already charged. Settlement compares what the order really cost with the standard cost of the good units; the difference is a variance, posted to its own account so you can study it. Scrap is production that failed and is thrown away.

How to create a production order by hand

  1. Click Production in the left sidebar.
  2. Click New production order.
  3. Choose the item to make, type the quantity and the start date (and finish date if you know it).
  4. Click Create order. Orders made from planning are already in the list.

How to release an order

  2. Click Release and confirm. (If the order is above the approval threshold the teacher clicks Approve and release.)

How to issue material to an order

  1. On the order's page scroll to the materials.
  2. Set the date first: the next click posts immediately.
  3. Leave the quantities as proposed and click Issue material to the order. Stock goes down and work in process goes up.
NOTE If you posted the wrong date, use Reverse on that document and issue again.

How to confirm production (post the work done)

  1. On the order's page scroll to the operations and click Confirm production.
  2. Choose the operation. Type the good units and scrapped units (and why, if any were scrapped).
  3. Type labor, machine and setup hours, or click the button that uses the planned hours.
  4. On the last operation tick the final-confirmation box.
  5. Set the date and click Post confirmation. Repeat for each operation.

How to put finished goods into stock

  1. On the order's page find Finished goods into stock.
  2. Type the quantity, choose where it goes Into (use the QUARANTINE store for an item inspected when made), and fill lot number and expiry if lot-tracked.
  3. Click Receive into stock.

How to settle and close an order

  1. When all units are received click Settle order.
  2. Check the settlement date and click Settle this order. What is left in work in process is written to the variance accounts.
  3. Then click Close order and confirm to lock it.

How to correct a mistake

  1. To undo a confirmation or a document find it in the documents list, click Reverse, type the date and reason, and confirm. Then post the right one.
  2. To undo a settlement click Undo settlement. To abandon an order that has not started click Cancel order and confirm.

Shop Floor

The day-to-day view for supervisors and operators: what to work on, how machines performed, and what stopped them.

Dispatch

The work list per work centre, in priority order, with the quantity still to make and the hours left.

What is the dispatch list?

The dispatch list is the supervisor's work queue for each work center: which operations are ready to run, in priority order, with the quantity and hours still to do. Changing an order's priority moves it up or down the list.

How to read the dispatch list and set priorities

  1. Click Shop Floor in the left sidebar.
  2. The Dispatch list tab shows operations ready to run; use the work-centre filter.
  3. To change the order of work type a new priority number for the order.
  4. Click Confirm on a ready operation to jump to the order and post it.

Downtime

Record stoppages with a reason and hours, so lost time is visible.

What is downtime?

Downtime is time a machine could have run but did not: breakdown, waiting for material, power failure. Logging it with a reason makes lost time visible and lowers the machine's availability in OEE.

How to log downtime

  1. Click Shop Floor in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Downtime tab.
  3. Choose the work centre, the day, the hours stopped and the reason. Add a note if you like.
  4. Click Log it.
  5. To cancel a wrong entry click Void on it, type why and confirm.

OEE

Overall equipment effectiveness: availability × performance × quality for each machine.

What is OEE?

OEE (overall equipment effectiveness) is one number that shows how well a machine is used: availability (was it running when it should?) × performance (did it run at full speed?) × quality (were the units good?). 100% would mean perfect production. The biggest loss tells you which of the three to fix first.

How to read OEE

  1. Click Shop Floor in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the OEE tab and choose how far to look back.
  3. Read availability, performance and quality per work centre; the biggest loss names the weakest.

Machines & upkeep

Machine register and upkeep (maintenance) tasks. Linking a machine to a work centre lets the system charge machine cost at real depreciation.

What is machine upkeep?

Upkeep (maintenance) keeps machines working. Preventive service is done on a schedule, for example every 500 machine hours, before anything breaks. A repair is done after a failure. Linking the machine to a fixed asset lets the system charge machine cost at the machine's real depreciation.

How to record machine service

  1. Click Shop Floor in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Machines & upkeep tab (the machine must exist under Fixed Assets first).
  3. To plan service click Plan, choose the fixed asset, type the service interval and click Save plan.
  4. After the work click Service, choose preventive service or repair, set the date and click Record.

Quality

Inspect what comes in and what goes out, and follow up failures until they are closed.

Inspection lots

Created automatically from receipts and production. Record the result and the decision: accept, scrap or return to vendor. A scrap or return writes its stock document and voucher.

What is an inspection lot?

An inspection lot is a batch of goods waiting to be checked. It is created automatically when an inspected item is received from a vendor or finished in production. Until the decision is recorded the goods sit in quarantine. The decision splits the lot: accept (goes into usable stock), scrap (thrown away, loss posted), return to vendor, or rework (sent back to be fixed).

How to inspect a lot and decide

  1. Click Quality in the left sidebar.
  2. On the Inspection lots tab click the lot that is waiting.
  3. Type the measured value for each characteristic and click Save measurements. The result shows pass or fail.
  4. Split the lot into accept into stock, scrap, return to vendor and rework; the quantities must add up to the lot.
  5. If anything is rejected choose the root cause. Set the date and type a note (required if you accept a result outside the limits).
  6. Click Record decision. Accepted goods move to the usual store; scrap and returns post to the books and raise a report.

Inspection plans

What to check for each item and the limits.

What is an inspection plan?

An inspection plan lists what to measure on an item, called characteristics, and the acceptable minimum and maximum. For lantern paper it might be moisture between 5% and 8%. A measurement outside the limits is a failure. The plan also says whether the item is inspected when bought, when made, or both.

How to set up an inspection plan

  1. Click Quality in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Plans and settings tab.
  3. Choose the item. Tick bought (inspect on arrival) and/or made (inspect when produced), then click Save these settings.
  4. Click Add a characteristic; type its name (for example Moisture), unit, minimum and maximum. Click Remove to delete a row.
  5. Click Save the plan. From now on that item must be received into the QUARANTINE store.

Non-conformance reports

Open a report for a defect, record the root cause and what happened to the goods, and use Close the report when finished. The loss in pesos is shown for each.

What is a non-conformance report (NCR)?

An NCR is the written record of a defect: what was wrong, how many units, the root cause (why it happened) and what was done with the goods. It stays open until you write the corrective action, the step taken so the problem does not repeat, and then close it. The peso loss is shown so the cost of poor quality is visible.

How to close a non-conformance report

  1. Click Quality in the left sidebar.
  2. Click the Non-conformances tab and click the open report.
  3. Type what was done to stop it happening again in the corrective action box.
  4. Click Close the report.

What is a delivery and the customer invoice?

A delivery (shipment) moves finished goods out of stock to the customer. The books record the cost of goods sold (COGS): the cost of what you gave away. The invoice is the bill to the customer. Finance

The ledger. Every posting in the books is a numbered journal voucher (JV-2026-00001…). Documents write theirs automatically; you prepare the adjusting ones.

Journal vouchers

Filter by date, type, status and source. Click a voucher to open the printable version.

Preparing a voucher

  1. Click New journal voucher.
  2. Set the date, period, kind and a reference.
  3. Write the particulars: what the entry is for and why.
  4. Add at least two lines with an account and an amount. Debits must equal credits.
  5. Save draft to continue later, or Submit / post voucher.

What is a journal voucher?

A journal voucher is the basic unit of the books: one entry with at least two lines, each a debit or a credit to an account. The total debits must equal the total credits. Documents such as receipts and invoices write their vouchers automatically; you prepare a voucher yourself only for adjusting entries.

Separation of duties means the person who prepares a voucher must not be the one who approves it. A voucher touching a control account (such as accounts receivable, accounts payable or inventory) is blocked unless it is an override with a written reason, and it needs approval. A posted voucher is never deleted; you reverse it.

How to post a journal voucher

  1. Click Finance in the left sidebar.
  2. On the Journal vouchers tab click New journal voucher.
  3. Set the voucher date and kind; type a reference and the particulars.
  4. For each line choose the account (type a code or a name), then type the debit or credit. Click Add line for more lines.
  5. Debits and credits must be equal. Click Submit / post voucher; it posts at once.
  6. To keep it for later click Save draft instead.

How to post a voucher that needs approval

  1. A voucher to a control account (accounts receivable, payable, inventory) or above the approval limit needs a second person.
  2. Tick the system-override / audit-adjustment box and type the reason (at least ten characters).
  3. Click Submit for approval.

How to approve, send back or reverse a voucher

  1. A teacher, or in a team company the Finance manager (never the person who prepared it), opens the voucher from the register.
  2. Click Approve and post to post it, or Send back and type what to fix.
  3. To undo a posted voucher click Reverse voucher, set the reversal date, type the reason and confirm. Then post the corrected voucher.

What is the chart of accounts?

The chart of accounts is the numbered list of every account the books use: cash, receivables, inventory, payables, sales, expenses. Each account has a code, a name and a type (asset, liability, equity, income or expense). The type decides which financial statement it appears on.

Reconciliation means checking that the bank's record and your books agree. Each bank line should match an entry in the books with the same amount. What is a fixed asset and depreciation?

Used in the activities. A machine registered here is linked to a work center, and this is how the system charges machine cost at real depreciation each month.

A fixed asset is something the company owns and uses for years, such as a machine. Its cost is not an expense at purchase; instead the cost minus the salvage value (what it is worth at the end) is spread over its useful life as depreciation. What is a stock card?

A stock card lists every movement of one item in date order with a running balance, like a bank statement for stock. Use it to find why a quantity is different from what you expected.

How to find a stock card

  1. Choose Stock card, choose the item and set the dates.
  2. Read each movement with its document, quantity, unit cost and running balance.

Books integrity check

What is the books integrity check?

The integrity check is a list of automatic proofs that the ledger and the sub-ledgers agree: stock records against the inventory account, receivables against customers, payables against vendors. Every row should pass. A failure points to the area where a posting is wrong; fix it before closing the month.

How to run the books integrity check

  1. Choose Books integrity check. Every row should say pass.
  2. A failed row names the area; fix the cause before you close the month.

What is a team company and roles?

In a team company the work is split between students by role. The system enforces it: a Purchasing officer can create purchase orders but cannot post a payment, a Warehouse clerk can receive goods but cannot approve vouchers. The teacher can change members and roles at any time, so a team keeps working even when someone is absent.

How to create a team company (teacher)

  1. Click Team companies → on the Teacher page.
  2. Click New team company.
  3. Type the company name, set the fiscal year start (use 2026-01-01) and the costing method (standard costing).
  4. Click Create. Leave the company empty.

How to add students and give roles (teacher)

  1. Open the team's row under Team companies.
  2. Choose the student and click Add to the team.
  3. Tick the roles that student holds (Purchasing, Warehouse, Production, Quality, Sales, Accountant, Finance manager, Auditor).
  4. Click Save roles. You can change members and roles at any time.
  1. Click to create a team company and name it.
  2. Add students and tick their roles.
  3. Open the books of the team at any time to check their work.

Teacher: comparing every company

The class comparison puts every Manufacturing company side by side: sales, margin, inventory, good units, scrap %, variances, months closed and whether the books tie. Click a column to sort, or jump to a company's Analytics or Period end.

How to work as a team member

  1. Open the team company from the company switcher. Your roles show under your name.
  2. Do only the steps of the roles you hold. If you try another one the system names the role you would need.
  3. Approvals (vouchers, held vendor invoices) are done by a different person from the one who prepared them.
19 · PUTTING IT TOGETHER

Month in the Life: a Worked Cycle

The order of work for one month, with where to do each step.
  1. Master Data: items, bills of materials, routings, work centers, rates.
  2. Costing: estimate the cost of each product.
  3. Planning: run requirements; convert planned orders and requisitions.
  4. Purchasing: purchase order → receive → vendor invoice.
  5. Quality: inspect receipts and decide.
  6. Production: issue materials, confirm operations on the Shop Floor, receive finished goods.
  7. Shipping: deliver and invoice.
  8. Sales and Purchasing: record customer payments and pay vendor invoices.
  9. Finance and Fixed Assets: post wages by voucher and record the machine's monthly depreciation.
  10. Finance: adjusting vouchers.
  11. Period End: settle, close variances, close the month.
  12. Reports: print the statements and the integrity check.

Troubleshooting and FAQ

The most common questions.
ProblemWhat to do
I cannot see Manufacturing ERP.Your teacher has not opened it yet.
I cannot press Approve on my voucher.By design: someone else must approve it.
Stock issue refused.Stock would go below zero. Receive or adjust stock first.
Vendor invoice on hold.It differs from the order or receipt beyond the tolerance; the teacher decides.
The month will not close.Run Books integrity check and settle open production orders first.
Reports do not agree with each other.They should always agree. Run the integrity check and tell your teacher.
I posted something wrong.Do not delete it. Reverse the voucher or the document; both stay on record.
